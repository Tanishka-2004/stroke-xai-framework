A Calibrated, Cost-Sensitive, and Explainable Machine Learning Framework for Stroke Risk Prediction on Imbalanced Clinical Data

Registration No: RA2311003011560
Department of Computer Science and Engineering, SRM Institute of Science and Technology
Research Domain: Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) for Stroke Prediction
Dataset: Official Kaggle Healthcare Stroke Prediction Dataset (N = 5,110)
Abstract—Cerebrovascular accidents (strokes) represent the second leading cause of global mortality and the third leading contributor to long-term cognitive and functional disability. While supervised machine learning algorithms demonstrate competitive discriminative potential in identifying vascular vulnerability, clinical deployment is obstructed by three interconnected challenges: (1) extreme class imbalance (<5% positive prevalence in community screening cohorts), (2) miscalibrated probability outputs caused by aggressive cost-weighting or oversampling, and (3) black-box opacity that precludes verification against established pathophysiological guidelines. Instead of relying on an isolated algorithm, this paper presents a calibrated, cost-sensitive, explainable stroke-risk framework structured across five rigorous clinical stages: (1) leakage-free median BMI imputation, (2) cost-sensitive CatBoost symmetric boosting with 19.54:1 class balancing, (3) 5-fold out-of-fold Platt probability calibration, (4) game-theoretic TreeSHAP feature attributions and non-linear bivariate interaction analysis (Age × Glycemia), and (5) multi-dimensional reliable evaluation including subgroup fairness and Decision-Curve Analysis (DCA). Evaluating on the Kaggle Stroke Prediction cohort (N = 5,110, 249 stroke events), the cost-sensitive CatBoost classifier achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.8402, a PR-AUC of 0.2812, and a minority-class sensitivity of 84.00% (identifying 42 of 50 test strokes). Crucially, Platt calibration reduced the Brier score loss from 0.1703 to 0.0416 (a 75.6% error reduction), aligning mean predicted risk (4.92%) with observed prevalence (4.87%). Decision-Curve Analysis demonstrates superior net clinical benefit across decision thresholds from 5% to 35%. This framework provides a rigorous, publication-grade blueprint for transparent, risk-calibrated cardiovascular screening.
Index Terms—Stroke Risk Prediction, Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), CatBoost, Cost-Sensitive Learning, Probability Calibration, Platt Scaling, Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), Decision-Curve Analysis (DCA), Class Imbalance.

I. Introduction

Stroke remains an acute neurovascular catastrophe responsible for approximately 6.6 million annual deaths and over 143 million disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) lost globally [1]. In ischemic stroke, cerebral arterial occlusion precipitates rapid, irreversible neuronal necrosis within the ischemic penumbra, progressing at an estimated rate of 1.9 million neurons per minute [2]. Consequently, primary preventative screening to intercept modifiable vascular risks prior to tissue infarction represents the cornerstone of modern neuro-epidemiology.

Traditional epidemiological risk scores, such as the Framingham Stroke Risk Profile (FSRP) [3] and the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score [4], employ linear additive heuristics with fixed point attributions. Although easily calculated at the bedside, these tools fail to capture higher-order non-linear interactions—such as the synergistic atherogenic acceleration observed when chronic hyperglycemia co-occurs with uncontrolled systolic hypertension in aging vessels [5].

To transcend linear limitations, medical researchers have increasingly adopted supervised machine learning algorithms, notably Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDT) [6]. However, standard clinical implementations suffer from three foundational vulnerabilities:

1) Severe Minority-Class Scarcity: Community and registry screening cohorts exhibit extreme class imbalance, typically containing 2% to 7% positive stroke events. Standard cross-entropy minimization causes models to collapse toward the majority class, producing deceptive ~95% accuracy while missing fatal vascular events [7].

2) Distorted Risk Calibration: Applying naive cost-sensitive weighting or synthetic minority oversampling (e.g., SMOTE) effectively forces the classifier to attend to minority cases, but severely shifts predicted probabilities upward. A patient with an actual 6% biological risk may receive an uncalibrated risk score of 65%, triggering patient panic and overtreatment [8].

3) The Black-Box Dilemma: Complex tree ensembles operate as uninterpretable black boxes. Clinicians and regulatory bodies cannot verify whether an individual prediction is governed by sound pathophysiological drivers or confounding dataset artifacts [9].

To systematically resolve these challenges, this paper presents a 5-Stage Calibrated, Cost-Sensitive, Explainable Stroke-Risk Framework evaluated on the official benchmark Kaggle Healthcare Dataset Stroke Data (N = 5,110).

II. Related Work & Methodology

A. Algorithmic Class Imbalance Handling

Class imbalance in clinical screening datasets (where positive stroke cases Y = 1 are rare) has conventionally been addressed via data-level resampling techniques like SMOTE [10]. However, Blagus and Lusa [11] proved that in tabular clinical settings, SMOTE artificially inflates local variance and distorts empirical class prior distributions, rendering posterior probability estimates invalid.

Conversely, algorithm-level cost-sensitive learning modifies the empirical risk objective directly:

ƒcost(y, ŷ) = − [ w1 · y log(ŷ) + w0 · (1 − y) log(1 − ŷ) ] (1)

Where class weights are parameterized inverse to class frequency: w1 = N / (2 · N1) and w0 = N / (2 · N0), establishing an exact balancing ratio of 19.54:1 for the Kaggle dataset.

B. CatBoost: Symmetric Decision Trees

CatBoost [12] offers distinct mathematical advantages over standard XGBoost and LightGBM for tabular clinical records containing mixed categorical and continuous variables. CatBoost avoids target leakage during categorical encoding by employing Ordered Target Statistics computed over pseudo-time permutations. Furthermore, CatBoost utilizes Oblivious (Symmetric) Decision Trees, where the exact same split criterion is applied across all nodes at a given depth. This serves as an intrinsic structural regularizer, significantly reducing overfitting on small, noisy clinical cohorts.

C. Post-Hoc Probability Calibration

A classifier is well-calibrated if its predicted probability equals the empirical event frequency. Platt Scaling [13] maps uncalibrated outputs into true posterior probabilities using a parametric logistic transformation:

P̂Platt(Y = 1 | f) = [ 1 + exp(A · f + B) ]−1 (2)

Where scalar parameters A and B are optimized via maximum likelihood on independent out-of-fold validation sets.

III. Proposed 5-Stage Framework

To overcome class imbalance (19.54:1 ratio), uncalibrated risk inflation, and black-box opacity, the proposed framework is structured into five sequential, leak-free operational stages evaluated on the Kaggle Stroke Dataset (N = 5,110):

Stage 1
Clinical preprocessing
Median BMI imputation (28.1), outlier removal, leakage-free 80/20 split.
Stage 2
Cost-sensitive CatBoost
19.54:1 class weighting, symmetric trees, native categorical handling.
Stage 3
Probability calibration
5-fold OOF Platt sigmoid scaling. Brier 0.170 → 0.041.
Stage 4
SHAP interpretability
Global rankings, patient waterfall plots, age × glucose interaction.
Stage 5
Reliable evaluation
Demographic parity audit, decision-curve analysis.

V. Explainable AI & SHAP Attributions

Using game-theoretic TreeSHAP [14], each patient prediction is decomposed into additive attributions:

f(x) = φ0 + ∑j=1M φj(x) (3)

Global feature importance rankings identify the primary drivers of stroke vulnerability: age (mean |φ| = 1.3975), bmi (0.2056), avg_glucose_level (0.0921), and hypertension (0.0806). Conversely, non-clinical features like ever_married contributed 0.0000, confirming regularization efficacy.

Interaction Analysis: Bivariate SHAP interaction dynamics confirmed non-linear synergy between age and glycemia. While elevated glucose (>180 mg/dL) in patients <45 yr adds only +0.08 log-odds, the same glucose level in patients >65 yr adds +0.46 log-odds (a 5.75× hazard increase), reflecting aged microvascular endothelial vulnerability.

VII. Conclusion

This research demonstrates a rigorous, five-stage calibrated and explainable machine learning framework for stroke prediction on imbalanced clinical cohorts. By integrating Cost-Sensitive CatBoost, Out-of-Fold Platt Scaling, and TreeSHAP explainability, the framework achieves 84.00% sensitivity, 0.8402 ROC-AUC, 0.0416 Brier score, and positive clinical net benefit across decision thresholds from 5% to 35%.
